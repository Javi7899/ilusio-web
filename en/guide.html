How to learn magic from scratch: first tricks and how to practise them

Which tricks to learn first, how to practise moves so they do not show, and the mistakes that give every beginner away.

Start with self-working tricks

Your first tricks should not depend on skill: the key card, the twenty-one card trick, the rubber pencil or the jumping band. They work the first time and teach the most important thing, which is how to present an effect.

Then the basic moves

With cards: shuffles and controls, the pinky break, the double lift and forcing a card. With coins: the French drop and the retention vanish. Practise them slowly in front of a mirror until you no longer think about them.

Practising in front of a mirror

The mirror shows you what the audience sees. But rehearse without it too: people look at your eyes, and if you look at your hands, so will they. Ten minutes a day is enough; what you do not repeat on different days, you forget.

Presentation beats the secret

A simple trick told well fools people more than a hard one done in silence. Write down what you will say, mind your angles (who is beside you) and know what to do if something goes wrong.

Beginner mistakes

Repeating a trick to the same person, explaining how it works, performing it before you have mastered it and going on too long. Always end on your strongest effect.
